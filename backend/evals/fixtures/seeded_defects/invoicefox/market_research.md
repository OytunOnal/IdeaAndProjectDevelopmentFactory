# InvoiceFox — Market Research

## Market sizing
- **TAM:** ~90M freelancers across US+EU; invoicing/billing software spend in the segment estimated at $2.1B/yr (confidence: medium — mixes SMB and solo figures).
- **SAM:** solo freelancers who invoice clients directly (excludes marketplace-mediated work): ~28M people; software-addressable spend ~$600M/yr.
- **SOM (36 months, realistic):** design/dev/writing freelancers in English-speaking markets reachable via community channels: ~1.2M; at low single-digit penetration and blended $8/mo ARPU, an obtainable ~$3-6M ARR ceiling for a focused player.

## Trends
- Freelance workforce growing ~6%/yr; late-payment complaints are persistent (surveys consistently show 40-60% of freelancers experience late payment in any given year; confidence: high on direction, medium on magnitude).
- Payment infrastructure (Stripe links, instant payouts) is commoditizing — differentiation is shifting to workflow around payments, not the rails.
- Headwind: all-in-one platforms (invoicing + accounting + banking) bundle aggressively at freemium price points.

## Target customer validation
- Primary segment: solo creative/technical freelancers, 3-20 invoices/month, income volatility makes each late payment materially painful.
- Validated pains (from community threads and rate-guide surveys): discomfort with confrontation when chasing; forgetting follow-ups; not knowing when an invoice was even seen.
- Willingness-to-pay indicators: freelancers already pay $10-20/mo for single-purpose tools (scheduling, proposals) when the tool touches income directly.

## Timing
Payment-behavior data is accessible (email opens, link clicks, Stripe events) and LLM-drafted messaging is now table stakes — a polite-escalation product is buildable by a tiny team today, and incumbents treat reminders as a checkbox feature rather than a craft.

## Verdict
A focused wedge (polite collections for solo freelancers) can win a niche the all-in-ones underserve; ceiling is a lifestyle-to-small business unless it expands into adjacent cash-flow tooling.

VIABILITY: moderate
