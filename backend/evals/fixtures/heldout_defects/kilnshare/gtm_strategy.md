# KilnShare — Go-to-Market Strategy

## Positioning
"Use a $5,000 machine for $12 an hour — or make yours pay for itself." Access economics for makers, with the safety and escrow rails that Facebook groups and generalist rental apps don't have.

## The cold-start plan (supply first)
A two-sided marketplace dies in the empty middle; we seed **supply-side first, in exactly two metros** with dense maker scenes. Target: 120 listed machines across the two cities before any renter-side marketing spends a dollar. Owner pitch leads with idle-asset math (a kiln used twice a month can clear $150-250/month listed).

## Launch phases
- **Months 1-2:** supply seeding — makerspace partnerships (their waitlists are our renter demand later), craft-fair owner outreach, hand-onboarding the first 50 machines
- **Month 3:** renter-side open in metro 1; makerspace waitlist partnerships convert
- **Months 4-6:** metro 2 opens once metro 1 clears 60% listing-utilization; referral loop launches

## Acquisition channels
1. **Makerspace partnerships** — their capacity waitlists become our demand; in exchange, spaces list their off-hours machines and take the owner side of the fee
2. **Craft fairs & guild networks** — where owners physically are; a table and a live payout demo outperforms ads in this community
3. **Metro maker Facebook/Discord groups** — the incumbent "channel" we're replacing; we participate honestly (escrow + safety gate as the upgrade pitch, not spam)
4. **Maker creators** — Instagram/YouTube build-video sponsorships; every rented tool tagged #kilnshare in the final cut

## Targets
- 120 machines listed pre-launch; 400 by month 12
- **$120k monthly GMV by month 12** at the standard **15% take rate** (10% renter + 5% owner, aligned with the PRD)
- Blended renter CAC under $20 by month 6; 70% of listings booked ≥1× in first month
