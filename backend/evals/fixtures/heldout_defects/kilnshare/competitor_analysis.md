# KilnShare — Competitor Analysis

## Direct & adjacent competitors
- **Fat Llama (now Hygglo)** — generalist P2P rental covering everything from drones to speakers. Proves P2P rental mechanics work, but a horizontal catalog means no safety gating, no category-specific handover flows, and dangerous equipment sits ungoverned next to party gear. Their fee structure is comparable (~15-25% blended), validating the take-rate band.
- **ShareGrid** — camera-gear P2P rental. The strongest analogy: a vertical that won by out-trusting the generalists with gear-specific verification and insurance. ShareGrid's playbook (vertical trust > horizontal reach) is the strategic template — nobody has run it for maker equipment.
- **Makerspaces** — the incumbent access model at $50-150/month. Strengths: induction culture, community, on-site supervision. Weaknesses: fixed hours, waitlists on kilns/lasers, and geography. Partial partner potential: off-hours listing of their machines.
- **Metro Facebook/Discord maker groups** — the real incumbent. Free, liquid, zero trust rails: no escrow, no deposits, no safety vetting, recurring damage-dispute horror stories. KilnShare's pitch is these groups' workflow with the danger removed.

## Gap analysis
No player combines **vertical maker focus + escrow/deposit rails + safety certification for dangerous categories**. The generalists can't retrofit safety culture per category; makerspaces can't escape their fixed-cost geography; the informal groups can't offer protection at all.

## Defensibility notes
- Two-sided verification plus category safety badges create switching costs the informal channel can't match
- The damage-pool track record itself becomes a moat: credible protection pricing requires claims data nobody else in the vertical has
- Makerspace partnerships are both channel and defense — locking their off-hours supply locks the bottleneck assets

## Pricing landscape
$8-15/hour rentals against $50-150/month memberships puts the casual maker's break-even at 4-8 sessions/month — most hobbyists sit below it, which is the wedge.
