# KilnShare — Market Research

## Market size & shape
- The hobbyist maker population keeps growing off the back of the ceramics revival, cheap resin printing, and the laser-cut goods economy (Etsy sellers as semi-pro demand)
- Entry equipment is expensive and lumpy: kilns $2,000-6,000, CO2 lasers $3,000-8,000, quality resin/FDM setups $800-2,500 — while typical hobbyist usage is **2-6 sessions/month**
- Owner-side telemetry from maker forums and studio surveys suggests home machines sit idle **>90% of hours**; owners routinely describe them as "guilt furniture"
- Makerspace membership — the incumbent access model — runs **$50-150/month** with fixed hours, induction queues, and waitlists for exactly the machines KilnShare targets (kilns and lasers are the bottleneck assets everywhere)
- Willingness to pay clusters at **$8-15/hour** for kiln/printer access and $40-90/day for lasers, benchmarked against makerspace day-pass pricing

## Metro concentration
Maker demand is intensely local: two-thirds of activity concentrates in metros with active guild/studio scenes. A two-metro launch can plausibly reach 25-40k active hobbyists without national marketing.

## Demand signals
- "Kiln firing service" and "laser cutting near me" searches sustain year-round volume with December peaks (gift-making season)
- Metro maker Facebook groups already broker informal equipment access constantly — with no escrow, no safety vetting, and regular horror-story threads about damage disputes
- Etsy-seller forums cite equipment access as the #1 scaling blocker before "time"

## Key risks
- **Liability is the market's shadow**: a single serious injury or fire on a brokered rental would define the category; safety gating and waiver flows are not optional features
- Supply cold-start: owners list only if payout math and damage protection are immediately credible
- Seasonality is mild but real (Q4 peak, summer dip)
