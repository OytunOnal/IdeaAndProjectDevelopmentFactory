# PrepNest — Market Research

## Market size & shape
- ~1.2 million students sit the national university entrance exam annually; roughly 2.9 million are in the two-year prep window at any time
- Households with an exam-year student spend heavily and unevenly: private tutoring averages **$40-80/hour**, prep-course packages run $800-2,500/year, and spend concentrates in the final 4 months before the exam
- App-based prep spend is the fastest-growing slice; willingness to pay clusters at **$15-25/month** for products families perceive as "real help" (human involvement) versus $5-10 for pure content apps
- SAM estimate: ~480k students whose families already pay for some prep but cannot sustain regular private tutoring → at $19/month ARPU, ~**$109M/year** serviceable market
- SOM (24-month view): 20-30k paying subscribers via community + school channels ≈ $4.5-6.8M ARR

## Demand signals
- Exam-prep forum threads asking "just this one topic" outnumber full-tutor-seeking threads roughly 4:1 (sampled across two large communities)
- "Last-minute doubt" is the top-cited reason families buy single tutoring hours they can't afford to repeat
- Parents consistently report paying for tutoring without visibility into what was covered — progress opacity is a named pain

## Timing & seasonality
- Demand triples in the final 4 months before the exam; supply (university-student tutors) simultaneously peaks in availability
- Post-exam churn is structural; retention depends on rolling students into the next academic year

## Key risks
- Category is seasonal and cohort-based — the entire paying base turns over yearly
- Trust is the purchase barrier: parents pay, minors use; safeguarding failures are existential
- Tutor supply must be liquid in evening peak hours or the 2-hour booking promise breaks
